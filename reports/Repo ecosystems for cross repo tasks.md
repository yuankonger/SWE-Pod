# Mine Home Assistant, Bluesky and MCP next

Three ecosystems should be built after Saleor. **Home Assistant** (core and frontend, all Apache-2.0) has the highest yield: about 265 frontend PRs merged in 2026 cite a `home-assistant/core#` PR, the two sides usually merge on the same day or the next, both sides carry tests that run offline, and the frontend's PR template has a "Link to backend pull request" field to mine from ([frontend search](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/pulls?q=is%3Apr+is%3Amerged+merged%3A2026-06-01..2026-11-18+%22home-assistant%2Fcore%23%22), [frontend#55604](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/pull/55604)). **Bluesky** (atproto under dual MIT/Apache-2.0, social-app under MIT) has the contract closest to Saleor's: since August 2026 the client keeps a vendored copy of the server's lexicon schemas, checked in CI, and its feature PRs reach the 5k to 12k-line size of the Saleor dashboard side ([social-app package.json](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/package.json), [social-app #11933](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/pull/11933)). **The Model Context Protocol** (spec and SDKs under MIT, moving to Apache-2.0) supplies width: one contract, several independent SDK teams and a shared conformance suite that can grade every side, which is the shape the earlier Saleor research said a team needs ([spec LICENSE](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/blob/main/LICENSE), [conformance](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/conformance)). Spree (BSD-3 and MIT) is the permissive commerce fallback. Lago has the best contract of any candidate, a committed `schema.graphql` consumed through codegen, but both of its repos are AGPL-3.0 and need legal sign-off first ([lago-api LICENSE](https://github.com/getlago/lago-api/blob/main/LICENSE)). Two autumn-2026 benchmarks, WideSWE and CrossSWE, already mine this pattern at scale, and they show three things. Mining yields little: about one linked group in forty becomes a task. Frontier agents solve 40 to 60% of such tasks. And their task sets are now public, so a new environment has to de-duplicate against them and pick larger, multi-PR features than they do ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1), [CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)). All yields per year in this report are extrapolated from 2026 search counts and samples, and no candidate was built or run.

## Published miners keep one linked group in forty, and agents solve half of what survives

The recipe used for `customer_attributes_company` is no longer novel. **WideSWE** (Zhejiang/Tsinghua, 27 Sep 2026) took the top 200 GitHub organisations by stars and kept 103 active ecosystems. It analysed 1,729,171 PRs merged since January 2024. Of these, **109,233 (6.3%)** explicitly referenced another repository in the same ecosystem. Deduplication and checks left 4,437 candidate groups; a date cutoff left 2,188; manual review left 635; 240 were runnable; 192 had a fail-to-pass test in every target repo. The final set is **120 tasks**: 60 features and 60 fixes across 41 ecosystems ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1)). **CrossSWE** (SJTU and others, 1 Nov 2026) has 171 instances across 165 repos and 13 languages, mined from January 2025 to September 2026. 165 of them involve two repos. Its largest intent category after features is API, schema and protocol evolution, with 43 instances ([CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)). Both benchmarks start each repo at its parent commit, use the upstream merge as the reference and upstream tests as fail-to-pass and pass-to-pass checks. That is our design.

Three numbers from that work should set expectations. First, the funnel is steep: 120 of 4,437 candidate groups survived (**2.7%**). The two largest cuts are manual review, which rejected "incidental citation", and runnability, which only **38%** of manually valid groups passed ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1)). This matches the wider evidence that most cross-references are not causal: only 12.11% of PR–issue links in an FSE 2024 study were "fixes" ([FSE 2024](https://cs.ubc.ca/~bestchai/papers/fse24-pr-issue-graphs.pdf)). Second, the tasks are not hard enough on their own. WideSWE's best full-task success is 42.50% (Codex CLI with GPT-5.6-sol; the paper's own table gives 41.67% for the same row). CrossSWE's best pass@1 is **58.48%** (GPT-6.1 Sol), with Claude Sonnet 5.5 at 50.29% ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1), [CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)). WideSWE's reference patches have a median of 13 files and 416 lines. The Saleor task's reference is 4,393 core lines plus 14,293 dashboard lines, so the size class we need is an order of magnitude above theirs. In practice that means taking a whole multi-PR feature, not one linked pair. Third, the multi-agent signal is small but real. On 89 comparable WideSWE tasks, joint execution solved 40.45% against 35.96% for independent per-repo execution. In CrossSWE, cross-repo inconsistency was the top failure mode, at 29–44% of failures depending on the model ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1), [CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)). Failures at the seam are exactly where a team with a contract owner could beat a solo agent, so the hypothesis stays testable.

The older literature gives base rates but little on features. It covers 271 cross-project correlated bug pairs in scientific Python ([ICSE 2017](https://bibtex.github.io/ICSE-2017-MaCZZX.html)), 1,491 cross-package links in Astropy ([arXiv 2402.15081](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.15081)), and co-evolving service groups in 11 microservice systems ([JSS 2023](https://macsphere.mcmaster.ca/handle/11375/28661?mode=full)). Before 2026, nobody had studied feature-level co-change across repos as a source of tasks.

## Fifteen ecosystems ranked: few are permissive, truly split across repos and active

Most candidate platforms fail at least one of three basic tests.

**Many have become monorepos.** Appwrite archived its console on 21 Sep 2026 and moved it into the main repo. Argo CD absorbed gitops-engine. Keycloak's admin UI lives inside the server repo, and Medusa, Vendure and Twenty are monorepos ([appwrite/console](https://github.com/appwrite/console), [argo-cd](https://github.com/argoproj/argo-cd), [keycloak js/apps](https://github.com/keycloak/keycloak/tree/main/js/apps)).

**Some are split in name only.** Kubernetes client-go is "an automatically published staged repository", and Envoy's go-control-plane is synced from upstream on every commit ([client-go](https://github.com/kubernetes/client-go), [go-control-plane](https://github.com/envoyproxy/go-control-plane)). Ory's repos receive bot-synced commits from an internal monorepo and had no merged PRs after March 2026 ([kratos](https://github.com/ory/kratos/commits/master)).

**Others carry licences a lab may refuse.**
- Sentry and Relay are FSL ([sentry LICENSE](https://github.com/getsentry/sentry/blob/master/LICENSE.md)).
- n8n uses its Sustainable Use License ([n8n](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md)).
- Directus moved to MSCL-1.0 in 2026 ([directus](https://github.com/directus/directus/blob/main/license)).
- Invoice Ninja is under the Elastic License 2.0 and Akaunting under the BSL ([invoiceninja](https://github.com/invoiceninja/invoiceninja/blob/v5-stable/LICENSE), [akaunting](https://github.com/akaunting/akaunting/blob/master/LICENSE.txt)).
- Vendure is now GPLv3 or commercial ([vendure](https://github.com/vendurehq/vendure/blob/master/LICENSE.md)).
- Home Assistant's own docs repo is CC BY-NC-SA ([home-assistant.io](https://github.com/home-assistant/home-assistant.io/blob/current/LICENSE.md)).

Licences also drift over time. Bahmni was AGPL-3.0 until its 3.0.0 release on 17 Sep 2026 and is MPL-2.0 after it ([bahmni-core #354](https://github.com/Bahmni/bahmni-core/pull/354)). So the licence check has to read the LICENSE file at each task's start commit, not at HEAD.

## Build Home Assistant, Bluesky and MCP; hold Spree and Lago in reserve

**Home Assistant first, because it is cheap and has the highest yield.** The match to the Saleor recipe is close:
- **Linking:** every frontend PR has a "Link to backend pull request" field, so precise candidate pairs come straight from PR bodies, with no keyword guessing ([frontend#55742](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/pull/55742)).
- **Timing:** the sides usually merge on the same day.
- **Tests:** core blocks network access in tests through `pytest-socket` and can be run one integration directory at a time, and the frontend's hidden tests are usually one `test/data/*.test.ts` vitest file ([pyproject.toml](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/blob/dev/pyproject.toml), [package.json](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/blob/dev/package.json)).
- **Licence:** Apache-2.0 throughout the code repos.

The weaknesses are equally clear. There is no schema file, so each task needs a contract document derived from the core WebSocket handler's schema, much as the Saleor task starts `core` with the target GraphQL schema in place. And typical pairs are smaller than Saleor's. The fix is to chain a series into one task, for example the per-direction battery energy work (core#190224 plus #190980, frontend#55604 plus #55713). A three-repo option also exists: Z-Wave credential management across core, frontend and zwave-js-server-python, about 6.9k added lines, all merged in May 2026 ([core#168360](https://github.com/home-assistant/core/pull/168360), [frontend#51591](https://github.com/home-assistant/frontend/pull/51591)). The May date means a model trained in mid-2026 may have seen it, so it fits a calibration set rather than a reward set.

**Bluesky second, because its contract matches Saleor's.** Social-app removed its `@atproto/api` dependency in a 391-file PR and now vendors 341 lexicon files, with `lexicons:verify` running `lex install --ci`. Atproto then added installation from local `file://` URIs, which lets a task harness point the client at the server checkout ([social-app package.json](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/package.json), [social-app lexicons.json](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/lexicons.json)).

This gives the exact "fit" criterion the Saleor datasheet uses: the client's schema copy must state what the server serves. The features are large and recent. Notifications v2 alone is about 4.2k lines on the server and 20k on the client across two PRs, merged between 30 Sep and 12 Oct 2026 ([atproto#5568](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/pull/5568), [social-app#11913](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/pull/11913)).

The costs are a Postgres and Redis sidecar for the AppView tests, and a client whose end-to-end suite needs mobile simulators. The browser-level target that the Saleor task uses would have to come from the Expo web build, which this research did not check.

**MCP third, because it supplies width.** The earlier Saleor research concluded that the next task should be one contract with several independent consumers, each with its own oracle. MCP has that structure natively, and it adds a shared verifier:
- **Width:** a SEP merges into the spec, then the TS, Python, Go, C#, Rust, Ruby and PHP SDKs each implement it within two to eight weeks.
- **Shared verifier:** since May 2026 a standards-track SEP needs conformance tests to reach Final, so the conformance repo can grade every SDK against the same scenarios ([spec#2484](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/pull/2484), [conformance#275](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/conformance/pull/275)).
- **Best seeds:** SEP-2549 (cache hints) and SEP-2243 (HTTP header standardisation). Both put real behaviour in every SDK, with 400 to 2,000 lines per side ([go#915](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/go-sdk/pull/915), [ts#2327](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/typescript-sdk/pull/2327)).

Three cautions apply. Most SDK implementations merged in June and July 2026, close to current training cutoffs, and MCP is discussed so widely that recall is a real risk. So the contamination probe below must pass before tasks count toward reward. Some SEPs are trivial, such as deprecations (SEP-2577) or error-code swaps (SEP-2164), and need filtering out by size. And the company framing is a protocol vendor with SDK teams, not a product company. OpenFeature has the same width shape with stronger timing: one author merged one change into flagd and seven provider repos on the same day ([flagd#1909](https://github.com/open-feature/flagd/pull/1909)). It is the substitute if MCP's recall risk proves too high.

**Spree and Lago stay in reserve for the commerce domain.** Spree's company-addresses pair is a clean same-day example under permissive licences. But its contract is an npm SDK built inside the monorepo, so the start state must link a locally built SDK instead of the registry. Many of its pairs are also staggered: the purchase-order feature merged on 31 Aug in core and 16 Nov in the storefront ([spree#14527](https://github.com/spree/spree/pull/14527), [storefront#306](https://github.com/spree/storefront/pull/306)). Lago becomes first choice for billing tasks the moment AGPL is cleared. Temporal is the reserve for a polyglot infrastructure company: proto contract, Go server, Java and Go SDKs, and a Svelte UI. Its contract repo has no tests, so hidden tests would come only from the implementation repos.

Each ecosystem should become one simulated company. Splicing repos from different ecosystems would mean inventing the contract between them, and that breaks the premise that the code, tests and features are real.

## A six-stage miner, from cross-references to admitted tasks

**Stage 1: candidate pairs.** The signal with the highest precision is the GraphQL timeline item `CrossReferencedEvent` with `isCrossRepository: true`. It returns the source and target issue or PR, `referencedAt`, and `willCloseTarget` ([GitHub GraphQL docs](https://docs.github.com/en/graphql/reference/issues)). Page through merged PRs per organisation with `is:pr is:merged merged:>=` and `-author:app/...` ([GitHub search docs](https://docs.github.com/en/search-github/searching-on-github/searching-issues-and-pull-requests)). Keep pairs whose merge dates fall within about 30 to 45 days of each other.

For ecosystem-wide sweeps, GH Archive on BigQuery can regex `owner/repo#N` and PR URLs out of PR and comment bodies. Third-party guidance puts the cost at about $6.25 per TiB scanned after a 1 TiB free tier, and LIMIT does not reduce it ([guide](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/gadievron/raptor/github-archive)). Cross-references alone miss much of the yield, so each ecosystem's own index should be added:
- Home Assistant's backend-link field
- Jira-style keys in squash titles: O3-, BAH-, DHIS2-, MDL/MOBILE ([OpenMRS esm-core#1881](https://github.com/openmrs/openmrs-esm-core/pull/1881))
- SEP and gRFC numbers in titles
- identical PR titles across repos, as in OpenFeature
- Lago's ticket IDs
- Zulip feature levels ([changelog](https://github.com/zulip/zulip/blob/main/api_docs/changelog.md))
- the commits that update vendored contracts: lexicon syncs, `@spree/sdk` pin bumps, `go.temporal.io/api` pseudo-versions

**Stage 2: filters.** Drop bot authors and PRs that touch only generated code or lockfiles. Keep a vendored schema as the contract but strip codegen output from the reference so the harness regenerates it. Require at least one hand-written test change and substantive source in every repo; CrossSWE excludes docs-only and changelog-only sides ([CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)). Set a size floor of about 150 lines with new tests on at least two sides, and prefer multi-PR features in the Saleor size class.

When one side merged first, the start is each repo's parent of its earliest PR in the feature, and the reference is the union of all of them. Examples of the consumer merging first: Supabase's client merged three days before the storage server, and MCP's go-sdk#931 merged before its spec PR ([supabase-js#2659](https://github.com/supabase/supabase-js/pull/2659), [go#931](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/go-sdk/pull/931)).

**Stage 3: execution gates.** Multi-SWE-bench's transition rules are the baseline:
- Run the suite three ways: base, base plus tests, base plus tests plus fix.
- Discard any test that goes from passing to failing.
- Require at least one test that goes from failing to passing ([Multi-SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02605)).

None of the cross-repo papers discusses flaky tests. Running base and reference at least three times and keeping only stable tests is our own default, not a published rule.

Then apply CrossSWE's **all-partial-mixes check**. Build every one of the 2^N combinations of base and patched repos in one image. Keep a task only if the fail-to-pass tests fail in the all-base state and in every partial state, and pass only when every patch is applied ([CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)).

There is a subtlety for our design that the paper does not face. Per-repo unit tests mock the other side, so a dashboard or social-app unit test will usually pass with only the client patched. The check therefore has to run on the cross-repo criteria: a browser or end-to-end target, a conformance scenario, or the schema "fit" check against the live server. A pair that passes in a partial mix on those criteria is a single-repo task and should be filed as one.

For spec-to-SDK families such as MCP and OpenFeature, the SDKs are independent of each other by design. The check runs per contract-and-SDK pair, and the width counts as parallel work, not coupling.

**Stage 4: de-duplication.** Match every candidate's repos and PR numbers against WideSWE's released instance list and CrossSWE's Hugging Face dataset ([WideSWE artifact](https://github.com/ZJU-ACES-ISE/WideSWE), [CrossSWE-Bench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CrossSWE/CrossSWE-Bench)). Do the same for single-repo sets that may contain one side of a pair: SWE-bench-Live, SWE-rebench, Multi-SWE-bench and FeatureBench ([SWE-bench-Live](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23419), [SWE-rebench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20411)). WideSWE drew its ecosystems from the top 200 organisations by stars, so large orgs like Home Assistant, Supabase and Temporal are likely in its pool. This research did not check which of them it used, so the overlap check is the first step, not an optional one.

**Stage 5: contamination.**
- **Recency:** use features merged after the target models' cutoffs. Snapshot any context repo no later than the earliest target base, as WideSWE does ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1)).
- **Network:** block network access to GitHub and the package registries during episodes.
- **Logs:** scan logs for attempts to read installed package source, as FeatureBench does ([FeatureBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10975v1)).
- **Recall probe:** adapt the published memorisation probe to cross-repo tasks. "The SWE-Bench Illusion" found models name the buggy file from the issue text alone 76% of the time on SWE-bench Verified, against 53% on other repos ([arXiv 2506.12286](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12286v1)). Microsoft's implementation treats exact path hits as the stronger signal ([BC-Bench](https://microsoft.github.io/BC-Bench/contamination-file-path-identification.html)). The analogue is to give the model only the brief for one repo and ask it to name the files, component names or PR number on the other side. A high exact-hit rate marks the task as recalled.

No published probe is designed for cross-repo tasks, so this one needs calibrating on the Saleor task, which the datasheet already flags as at risk of recall.

**Stage 6: test-coupling repairs.** WideSWE revised the tests in **88 of its 120 tasks**. It relaxed implementation-specific assertions, removed checks for functionality the task did not ask for, and re-verified that the revised suite still rejects the base and accepts the reference ([WideSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33382v1)). FeatureBench takes the other route and states the signatures and import paths the tests need in the prompt ([FeatureBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10975v1)). CrossSWE avoids prescribing names and relies on behavioural cross-repo tests ([CrossSWE](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01484)).

The Saleor datasheet already combines these, because it hit the same problem. Its `INTERFACE.md` names every file, export and string the dashboard tests need. Tests of upstream's own helpers are reported but not scored, the remaining dashboard tests weigh half, and a browser target checks what staff actually do. That template carries over unchanged. For Home Assistant and Bluesky, the client tests are as implementation-bound as Saleor's dashboard tests, so the interface document and a browser or end-to-end target are both needed. For MCP, the conformance scenarios can carry full weight because they test the wire behaviour only.

## Conclusion

The search changed what counts as the scarce resource. Cross-repo features are not rare: Home Assistant alone produces about one candidate pair every working day. What is scarce is the combination of a permissive licence, a real repo split, tests that survive both partial-mix gating and interface repair, and a feature large enough that frontier agents fail it. WideSWE and CrossSWE show that ordinary linked pairs land at 40 to 60% solved, so mining by link volume will mostly produce tasks that are too easy and already public. The leverage is in grouping several linked PRs into one Saleor-sized feature and in choosing ecosystems whose contract is a file both sides commit. A third route, MCP and OpenFeature, has one contract with many independent implementers, so an extra agent there means real parallel work rather than one feature divided in two.

There is a quieter implication for the hypothesis. The only published comparison, WideSWE's 4.5-point gain for joint over independent execution, comes from tasks smaller than ours and was not run at matched budget. The first few tasks from each new ecosystem should therefore be run in all four seats (solo, lead, team, team without messaging) before mining scales up. If solo still matches the team on Home Assistant's chained pairs, the evidence points to MCP-style width, or to a binding deadline, rather than to more pairs of the same shape.
